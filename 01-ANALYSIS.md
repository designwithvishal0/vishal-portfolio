# Senior review of designwvishal.framer.website

Reviewed 8 October 2026, desktop 1440 and mobile 390, home page plus the ixigo case study, with the lens of a design lead screening for a mid to senior product designer at a top product company.

## The verdict in one paragraph

The work is better than the site. The case study writing (ixigo, Split and Request, Twelve, JARVIS) is genuinely strong: it opens on a real tension, states the role high, and shows tradeoffs. But the home page wraps that work in a recognisable Framer template, so a hiring manager's first 10 seconds say "template portfolio" before they ever reach the thinking. The site also hides its single strongest piece of proof (real, shipped commerce results) and gives equal weight to everything, including graphic design work that pulls the positioning away from product design.

## What a hiring manager sees in the first 30 seconds

1. A centred pill tag, a two line headline with a rotating last word ("growth" / "engagement" / "conversions"), smoke gradient background, two pill buttons, "Scroll down to see projects" with a mouse icon, and a scrolling logo strip. Every one of these is a template default. Together they read as bought, not designed.
2. The headline "I design products that drive growth" is a claim every designer makes. It says nothing only Vishal could say.
3. On mobile, the rotating word animation catches mid frame and the headline reads "I design products that drive" with nothing after it.
4. A "Made in Framer" badge floats over the hero and every case study.
5. His photo is the largest tile in the work grid, bigger than any project, and the same photo appears again in "Meet Vishal".

## The work grid

* Every card is a poster image with a "View Project" pill over its bottom. There is no title, no one line outcome, no "concept" or "shipped" label, and no role in text. A recruiter cannot scan the grid; they have to read every poster.
* About 20 identical "View Project" buttons on one page. The button carries no information.
* Some cards go to a case study on the site and some leave for Behance, with no signal which. Sending a hiring manager off site mid scan loses them.
* The top five pieces are all self initiated concepts (ixigo, Google Pay Split, Twelve, JARVIS, Reward Centre). Concepts are fine, but with nothing shipped next to them a senior reviewer asks "has this person shipped anything?"

## The proof problem

Profit Wala Love has real results: 1,542 transactions, ₹9.89 lakh GMV, 13,707 shares, +51% retailer commission. That is the only hard business result on the site, and it sits as one poster in the second section with the number cropped inside an image. It should be one of the first things anyone sees.

## Hierarchy and range

The page runs: hero, logos, top case studies, Pages that sell, Meet Vishal with a tools list and experience table, UI explorations carousel, Graphic design carousel, footer. Every section has the same weight. Two things dilute the positioning for product roles:

* **The Graphic design section** (business cards, Eid banners, standees, social posts). Right for a brand designer portfolio, wrong on the home page of someone aiming at Product Designer roles. It belongs in an archive.
* **The tools list** of 13 chips (Figma, FigJam, Miro, ChatGPT, Midjourney…). Senior portfolios do not list tools; the work proves them.

## Case study pages (ixigo as the example)

What works: the hook ("The four hours nobody designs for"), the role, scope, type and year row near the top, the user flow, state by state sections with honest copy, trade offs and the design review learnings.

What holds it back:

1. The title appears twice in a row: once as a text hero, then again inside the video poster directly below.
2. It is about 18,000 pixels long with no table of contents, no progress indicator and no 30 second summary. A hiring manager with 3 minutes cannot find the decision that matters.
3. The layout is a stack of Framer sections with uneven spacing; screens float at a fixed width on the right with a lot of empty space on the left.
4. No "next case study" path that keeps a reader moving through the best work.

## SEO and technical

1. The JARVIS page carries Twelve's title, description and share image.
2. A duplicate "English (India)" copy of every page lives under /en/ and canonicals to itself, splitting ranking.
3. No structured data telling Google who he is. Searching "Vishal Yadav product designer" does not show the portfolio in the top 10; other Vishal Yadavs do.
4. The site sits on a framer.website subdomain, has hit Framer's 1 GB bandwidth limit, and shows the Framer badge. None of that is ownable.
5. The footer email (design.vishaly@gmail.com) differs from the CV email.

## What to keep

* The case study writing voice and structure. It is the best asset.
* The showreels with voiceover. Few portfolios at this level have them.
* The thumbnails' headline first approach ("Alerts worth waking up for", "The split that stays alive"). These lines are great; they just need to live as text too, not only inside images.
* "Pages that sell" as an idea: it is the bridge to freelance work. It needs proof and a clear ask, not more posters.
* The CTA to book a call.

## The three moves that matter most

1. **Replace the template hero with a specific one.** His name, what he does, one sentence only he could write, availability, and the work starting above the fold.
2. **Make the work scannable and the proof visible.** Each project shows title, one line outcome, role, concept or shipped, and a number where one exists. Shipped commerce results sit high.
3. **Cut to the work that serves the positioning.** Product case studies and commerce work on the home page, everything else in an archive.
